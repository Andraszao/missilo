Four ways Missilo could look

Each board draws the same four seconds: one satellite circling the planet, three threats falling out of the dark side, one shot, one burst. The drawings are live code, not finished art. They are here to compare feeling, palette and how each direction would show charge, danger and pickups without a single label.

All four keep the rules from the core prototype: no HUD, city lights as health, sunlight as ammunition.

Direction A

The Orrery

The game is a brass instrument on a desk. The planet is an enamel globe, the satellite rides a geared arm, the sun is a desk lamp, and threats are red glass marbles rolling in.

baizebrasspolishenamelrubylamp
Night the ThirdEngraved small capitals, as on a scientific instrument.
Charge
Six filament bulbs on the satellite's carriage. They glow in the lamplight and go dark in the globe's shadow.
Danger
City jewels on the rim go out one by one. The felt shows the globe's real cast shadow.
Pickups
New parts arrive as physical pieces: a gear, a second arm, a lens. You see them click onto the machine.

Cost: needs good metal and glass materials to sell it. Payoff: every upgrade is a visible part, so the run builds an object you can read at a glance.

Direction B

Little Lamp

A tiny painted planet with houses you could count. Gouache and chalk on dark paper. The satellite is a lantern, and what you are protecting is visibly somebody's home.

inksagepaperochrecoralmoss
The third nightSoft serif with a rounded companion. Reads as a picture book.
Charge
The lantern's flame. Bright in daylight, guttering in the planet's shadow.
Danger
Windows go dark house by house. A lost city leaves chimney smoke, not rubble.
Pickups
Parcels drift up from the houses on balloons. The town is sending you things.

Cost: hand-painted assets, and the glowing 3D blasts you liked would become painted blooms. Payoff: the strongest emotional hook of the four, and it is nothing like the genre's usual look.

Direction C

Two-Ink Print

Flat shapes in three spot inks on warm paper, with halftone for shadow and plates printed slightly out of register. Loud, graphic, instantly readable on a phone in daylight.

Night 03Heavy grotesque + typewriter mono. Poster energy.
Charge
Yellow dots round the satellite. In shadow they print as empty outlines.
Danger
Yellow city blocks drop out of the print, leaving bare paper.
Pickups
Each item adds a shape to the satellite's silhouette. More inks unlock as the run goes on.

Cost: a light background gives up the night mood, and flat ink gives up the 3D blasts. Payoff: cheapest to produce, clearest at small size.

Direction D · where the prototype is now

Night Light

Deep space, a lit globe, bloom on everything that matters. Handsome and safe. This is the look you called generic, and it is the baseline to beat.

My pick: the Orrery

It is the only direction where "everything lives in the world" is literal. The whole screen is one object, so there is nowhere a HUD could even go. Sunlight as ammunition becomes a desk lamp and a real shadow, which a player understands on sight.

It also keeps what you liked. Bursts stay as 3D light, and they get better: a flash that rakes across brass and glass is a stronger effect than a glow on black. Upgrades become parts bolted onto the machine, which is the Isaac idea of reading a run off the character's body.

Little Lamp is the one I would choose if the feeling you want is tenderness more than craft.
