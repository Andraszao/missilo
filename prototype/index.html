Director's notes · follows the game

    From the demo to the rebuild

    The Godot demo is a clean arcade homage with good bones: event bus, pooled projectiles, data-driven waves and a modifier system that nothing uses yet. This prototype is the experience I'd build on those ideas, with its own identity.

    Kept from the demo

    • Aim, fly, burst. The interceptor still detonates where you pointed, after a flight you have to lead.
    • Three silos, six things to protect. Same counts, same loss condition.
    • Chain reactions with shrinking generations. Same idea as incoming_missile.gd, tuned to carry further.
    • Speed Boost, Blast Radius, Extra Ammo. The three .tres modifiers, now one per starting silo.
    • Per-hour ammo with a refill between waves.

    Changed

    • One input per shot. Click fires the nearest silo; A, S, D fire a chosen one. No select-then-click.
    • Chains are the score, the sound and the meter. In the demo they were a side effect.
    • Waves are volleys with shapes. The demo spawned on a metronome at random x.
    • Four threat types instead of one. Each asks a different question.
    • A draft after every hour. The unused behavior_flag hook becomes Twin Rail, Aftershock, Recycler.
    • A hit silo is out for the hour, not the run. Losing one in wave 2 no longer decides wave 10.
    • A night with a dawn. Sky, moon and clock replace "Wave 7".

    Not in this prototype

    • Longer nights and route choice. Pick the next hour's threat mix for a better reward.
    • A final-hour set piece. One large target that sheds chain fuel.
    • Twenty-plus mods with synergies and rarities.
    • Between-night unlocks: new starting batteries, new valleys.
    • Real art, music that layers with chain depth, controller support, saves.

    What I need from you

    1. Does this direction feel right? Chain-first, build-per-silo, one night per run.
    2. Engine for the real build. I'd stay on Godot 4.5 with a 2D renderer; the demo's 3D scene adds cost and no gameplay. Web-first in TypeScript is the other sensible option.
    3. Target platform. The prototype already plays by touch; a phone-first build changes HUD and pacing choices.
    4. Run length. Six hours here takes about five minutes. A full game probably wants ten to twelve.
